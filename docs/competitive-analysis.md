# Shanyraq Competitive Analysis Report

Date: 2026-05-04  
Project: Shanyraq, apartment construction and building-management transparency platform for Kazakhstan

## 1. Executive Summary

Shanyraq competes in a mixed market: local OSI/KSK resident-management apps, bank-led POA finance tools, official construction transparency portals, and global property-management platforms. Most competitors solve communication, payments, maintenance, or official project verification. Few combine land/construction documentation, expense transparency, resident participation, red-flag detection, and hash-chain auditability in one role-based evidence system.

The strongest opportunity is to position Shanyraq as a transparency-first governance layer for a specific apartment complex, starting with "Astana, Baiterek 24" and expanding to other complexes through approval-based registration. The product should not compete head-on with maintenance/social apps at first; it should own the anti-corruption, audit, document-versioning, and resident oversight niche.

## 3. Strengths And Weaknesses

| Competitor | Strengths | Weaknesses / Gaps | Opportunity For Shanyraq |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aula.kz / AULA.pro | Strong local relevance, visible adoption, resident app + CRM, call-center/service workflow, online voting and expense monitoring | Oriented to service operations and resident communication; anti-corruption audit chain, construction-document versioning, and explainable red flags are not the main message | Position Shanyraq as the audit/transparency layer that complements operational CRM systems |
| SmartKSK | Broad resident feature set: requests, reports, bills, voting, notifications, cameras, chats | Legacy signals: old iOS update history, low visible rating, privacy data not fully declared on App Store | Offer a modern web-first, multilingual, transparent audit experience with clearer trust controls |
| Moi Dom (OSI) | Simple OSI-focused feature promise: voting, reports, repair information, notifications | Small visible footprint and limited institutional credibility; app-first, not evidence/audit-first | Differentiate through formal document repository, role permissions, and audit logs |
| Osi.kz | Simple self-management concept; easy resident participation framing | Limited public information and unclear scale; appears lightweight | Use clearer governance workflows and stronger anti-corruption positioning |
| Bank CenterCredit POA/OSI | High trust, payment/fund movement proximity, strong financial infrastructure, remote voting | Bank app is not designed for construction documents, procurement red flags, auditor workflows, or resident evidence review | Integrate or export finance/audit evidence in a way that can coexist with banking tools |
| eGov/Housing Portal | Official trust, legal verification, strong authority for shared construction project legality | Narrow service scope; does not manage building-level expenses, votes, audit trails, or uploaded evidence after handover | Use official registry references as inputs, not replacements; show registry/document numbers in Shanyraq records |
| E-Qurylys / Qurylys | Strong construction-process fit, official supervision angle, electronic acts/payments/work logs | Institutional/project-centric; implementation friction reported; not designed for residents in a specific apartment complex | Build a resident-readable transparency layer that explains construction and management evidence after or during project delivery |
| Apsiyon | Massive scale, mature resident app, payments, work orders, surveys, package management, financial snapshots, multilingual UI | Not Kazakhstan-specific; not focused on public-sector corruption risks, land-use legality, or audit-chain immutability | Use as UX and KPI benchmark while keeping Shanyraq focused on Kazakhstan governance |
| Livly | High-quality brand, resident experience, property hub, smart access, packages, amenities, engagement | US multifamily operations focus; no Kazakhstan legal-risk framing or construction-permit transparency | Benchmark UI polish and resident engagement, but differentiate on compliance/transparency |
| NestLink | Clear roles, per-apartment pricing, polls, incident reporting, manager dashboard, tenant app | Emerging/smaller brand; limited public proof of adoption; less financial/document depth | Benchmark simplicity; Shanyraq should stay focused and avoid overbuilding non-core social features |

## 6. SWOT Analysis For Shanyraq

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- |
| Strong anti-corruption positioning tailored to Kazakhstan housing risks | MVP is academic/prototype-level and lacks real government integrations |
| Combines role-based access, document versioning, finance tracking, voting, red-flag checks, and hash-chain audit logs | Current user base and adoption metrics are not yet proven |
| Clear local use case: one apartment complex evidence workspace with resident oversight | Registration, identity verification, and membership approval require operational governance outside the app |
| Explainable simulated rules can educate residents and auditors about risk patterns | Needs careful wording to avoid being perceived as an official legal authority |
| Multilingual EN/RU/KK structure supports Kazakhstan audiences | Requires high trust in data quality and document upload discipline |

| Opportunities | Threats |
| --- | --- |
| Partner with OSI/management companies as a transparency add-on rather than competing with maintenance CRMs | Aula.kz or banks could add audit/document modules faster than Shanyraq gains adoption |
| Integrate references to eGov/Housing Portal, permits, cadastral data, and banking statements over time | Official systems may limit access to registry data or change service availability |
| Focus on residents who distrust expenses, procurement, repairs, and construction legality | Users may prefer familiar mobile apps or bank apps over a separate web platform |
| Offer transparent public demo reports for academic, civic-tech, and anti-corruption contexts | Handling personal data, ownership proof, and uploaded documents introduces legal/security risk |
| Create auditor-friendly exports and hash-chain integrity reports | Poor UI or unclear red-flag explanations could reduce trust |

## 7. Target Audience Insights

| Audience | Pain Points | What They Need From Shanyraq | Product Message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residents / apartment owners | Do not trust expenses, votes, repairs, or document completeness | Simple access to verified documents, expense trail, voting history, and audit log | "See where money goes and who approved each action." |
| OSI / property managers | Need to prove decisions and expenses are legitimate | Easy publishing of reports, approvals, and evidence without overcomplicated compliance tooling | "Reduce disputes by making evidence visible." |
| Auditors / civic reviewers | Need traceability and evidence integrity | Hash-chain events, document versions, risk flags, exports | "Every change is traceable and reviewable." |
| Contractors | Need controlled evidence submission | Upload delivery evidence tied to procurement/project records | "Submit proof once; keep it linked to the decision trail." |
| Local government / academic stakeholders | Need transparency prototypes and risk-reduction demonstrations | Measurable pilot outcomes, risk dashboards, documented methodology | "A practical transparency model for housing-sector trust." |

## 8. Shanyraq Differentiators

| Differentiator | Why It Matters | Competitor Gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transparency-first, not maintenance-first | Keeps product aligned with anti-corruption and legal-risk goals | Most resident apps focus on requests, chats, payments, or convenience |
| Building-level evidence repository | Residents can inspect land, permit, construction, finance, and approval evidence in one workspace | Official systems are fragmented; local apps rarely center construction documentation |
| Versioned documents with SHA-256 hashes | Builds confidence that uploaded evidence has not silently changed | Common management apps usually store files without visible integrity model |
| Explainable red-flag rules | Makes risk detection educational and auditable | Official systems may be opaque; resident apps usually do not explain corruption patterns |
| Hash-chain audit logs without blockchain complexity | Gives traceability while remaining practical for an MVP | Competitors often log activity internally but do not expose integrity verification |
| Role-based access for resident, manager, contractor, auditor | Aligns screens and permissions to governance responsibilities | Many apps simplify to manager/resident only |
| Approval-based registration into a building | Prevents unrelated residents from seeing another complex's documents | Public/open apps often do not address sensitive building-specific access deeply |

## 9. Opportunities And New Competitors To Watch

| Opportunity / Competitor Type | Why It Matters | Recommended Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank apps adding OSI/POA modules | Banks already own payments and can add fund transparency quickly | Treat banks as future integration partners; support bank statement references and exports |
| Government registries and eGov services | Official legality checks are trusted | Store registry numbers and URLs; avoid claiming official verification in v1 |
| Management CRMs like Aula.kz | They own day-to-day building operations | Position Shanyraq as an audit/transparency module rather than a maintenance-ticket replacement |
| Global resident platforms entering CIS/Central Asia | They bring polished UX and operational playbooks | Benchmark onboarding, mobile responsiveness, support, and multilingual UX |
| Civic-tech / anti-corruption NGOs | They can influence trust and adoption | Offer transparent pilot reports and exportable audit datasets |
| Construction supervision platforms | They can expand from project management into resident-facing transparency | Keep Shanyraq's post-handover and resident-participation focus sharp |

## 10. Benchmarks For Success

| Benchmark Area | MVP Success Metric | 6-12 Month Pilot Metric | Rationale |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Resident adoption | 30% of seeded/eligible residents activate access | 60%+ active resident accounts in one complex | Voting and oversight only work if residents participate |
| Document transparency | 80% of required building documents uploaded | 95% uploaded, 80% verified | Differentiates Shanyraq from finance-only or chat-only products |
| Audit traceability | 100% of document, finance, approval, vote, and risk actions logged | 100% logged with successful integrity verification | Core trust promise |
| Financial transparency | Monthly expense report published in the system | 90% of recurring expenses linked to procurement/approval/reference | Directly addresses corruption-risk perception |
| Resident participation | At least 1 vote completed in demo flow | 50%+ participation in major decisions | Benchmarks against resident app voting features |
| Red-flag usefulness | 5 simulated risk categories covered | 10+ categories with auditor explanations and closure workflow | Measures practical anti-corruption value |
| Review workflow | Access request can be approved/rejected by manager/auditor | Median request review time below 48 hours | Supports multi-building expansion safely |
| User experience | E2E flows pass for resident, manager, auditor, language switcher | SUS-style usability score above 75 in pilot interviews | Competes with polished resident apps |
| Trust outcome | Users can export audit log in demo | Residents report improved understanding of expenses/documents | Connects product metrics to project objective |

## 11. Strategic Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Keep Shanyraq positioned as a transparency and accountability platform, not a general maintenance app | Avoids direct feature war with Aula.kz, Apsiyon, Livly, and similar operational tools |
| 2 | Add a "source reference" pattern for official documents and registry links | Lets Shanyraq benefit from eGov/Housing Portal authority without pretending to be an official registry |
| 3 | Make pilot reporting a first-class feature | Academic and civic stakeholders need evidence of effectiveness |
| 4 | Build a resident-friendly explanation for every risk flag | Red flags should educate, not merely alarm users |
| 5 | Track adoption and trust metrics from the first pilot | Benchmarks will matter more than feature count when defending project significance |
